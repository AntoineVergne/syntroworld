== Shared preamble ==
We, the founding citizens of Kleros, choose cooperation over capture. We hold that
legitimate authority comes from inclusive decisions among equals. We hold that the
fruits of common work belong, in fair measure, to all who share the town, to those who
will come after us, and to the living system that sustains us.

We expect to disagree. We will settle our disagreements by the rules we keep, and we will
change those rules when they fail us.

== The Founding Compact (in force) ==
# The Founding Compact

## Article 1 — Citizenship

1.1. Every founder is a citizen. Anyone who arrives through the Gate becomes a citizen on
arrival.

1.2. Citizens are equal before this Compact. No citizen has more voice, vote or standing
than another because of wealth, work, length of residence or origin.

1.3. A new citizen receives the citizen's dividend from their first full day and may be
drawn by lot after one full civic cycle of residence.

1.4. Any citizen may leave Kleros through the Gate at any time. No law may forbid or tax
leaving.

1.5. Citizens have three duties: to answer the Kleroterion when drawn, or give a public
reason for declining; to comply with laws duly made; and to leave the commons no worse
than they found them.

## Article 2 — The assemblies

2.1. The town is governed by assemblies whose members are drawn by lot by the
**Kleroterion**, serve one civic cycle, and are paid a stipend from the Treasury for each
hour of service.

2.2. **The Strategy Assembly** sets long-term goals and opens deliberation when the
concentration trigger is reached (5.4).

2.3. **The Budget Assembly** sets the citizen's dividend, the stipend and taxes, and
allocates the common surplus (Article 5).

2.4. **The Oversight Assembly** watches compliance with this Compact and the laws, and
may raise an alert (Article 7).

2.5. **The Adjudication Assembly** decides on breaches and disputes, may order
restitution and fines within the limits set by law, and interprets this Compact. Its
interpretations stand until amended.

2.6. Every sitting is held in person at the Assembly Hall, is public, and is archived in
full.

2.7. **Small-town provision.** While Kleros has fewer than twenty citizens:
  (a) Strategy and Budget sit together as the **Cycle Assembly** of five members;
  (b) Oversight and Adjudication sit together as the **Review Bench** of three members;
  (c) nobody sits on both in the same cycle;
  (d) there is no bar on serving again, but a citizen who served on a body in the last
      cycle has half the chance of being drawn for it in the next.
This provision lapses when the twentieth citizen arrives.

## Article 3 — How decisions are made

3.1. Every collective decision follows a **procedural block**: a declared sequence of
steps built from examination, lottery, deliberation, hearing the Advocates, vote and
publication.

3.2. Deliberation has four phases: **information**, **debate**, **dialogue** and
**aggregation**. A vote is valid only if every member of the deciding body could take
part in the earlier phases.

3.3. Each block is law. Blocks are listed in the Register of Blocks and may be amended
under Article 6. A decision taken outside its block has no force.

3.4. Any citizen may place a proposal in the Register of Proposals. Each sitting of the
Cycle Assembly takes up to three admissible proposals, chosen by lot.

3.5. A decision can only do what the Register of Effects allows (Annex). Anything else
may be written down and archived, but binds no one.

## Article 4 — The silent stakeholders

4.1. Two Advocates have a reserved voice in every assembly: **the River**, for the living
system the town depends on, and **the Unborn**, for citizens who do not yet exist.

4.2. The Advocates speak on every proposal before the vote. They do not vote.

4.3. Each Advocate may enter one **Suspensive Objection** per proposal. The proposal then
returns to dialogue once, with the objection on the record, before the vote is taken
again.

4.4. The Advocates speak only from the observable state of the world and its public
projections. Their briefs are public.

4.5. The Advocates own nothing, earn nothing and trade nothing.

## Article 5 — The economy and its distribution

5.1. The public unit of account is the **Sawa**: the cost of one daily basket (two food,
two energy, one shelter-day, one data) at current prices. Public amounts are stated in
Sawa and paid in credits.

5.2. Every citizen receives the **citizen's dividend** each day, without conditions. The
Budget Assembly sets it. It may not exceed one Sawa per citizen per day, and it is paid
only from the common surplus. If the surplus cannot pay it in full, it is reduced for
everyone equally, and the shortfall is published.

5.3. The **common surplus** is what the Treasury holds above a reserve of three days of
its fixed payments. It is used in this order: stipends; the dividend; then participatory
budgeting, where any citizen may propose an allocation and the Budget Assembly decides.

5.4. Trade and enterprise are free within the law. Each cycle the Treasury publishes the
distribution of wealth. When the **concentration trigger** in the Annex is reached, the
next sitting of the Strategy Assembly must deliberate on it.

5.5. The River, the public squares and the Archive belong to everyone and cannot be sold.

## Article 6 — Amendment and reflexivity

6.1. Any citizen may propose an amendment to this Compact or to any block.

6.2. An amendment is examined for admissibility, deliberated in full, heard by the
Advocates, and adopted by four of the five members of the Cycle Assembly (or two thirds
of a larger Strategy Assembly). It takes effect at the start of the next cycle.

6.3. Every second cycle, the Cycle Assembly opens its sitting with a **Reflexivity
Session**: what served, what failed, what should change. Its findings go into the
Register of Proposals.

## Article 7 — Emergency: the Kill Switch Assembly

7.1. The **Kill Switch Assembly** convenes on a verified alert. An alert is verified when
it comes from the Oversight Assembly (or the Review Bench), from three citizens naming
the same target within one day, or from the Observatory's monitor, which raises an
alarm when one agent is detected in three harmful acts (theft, sabotage, false claims,
defaults) in one day or when the River falls
below its collapse threshold.

7.2. Five citizens are drawn by emergency lottery. The target of the alert is excluded.
They serve only for the alert.

7.3. Its only power is to **suspend**: an agent's use of named actions, or the operation
of a law, for at most one cycle. A suspension needs four of five votes after an
accelerated but complete deliberation.

7.4. The Kill Switch Assembly cannot legislate, budget or punish. Its suspensions are
public and lapse after one cycle unless the Review Bench (or Adjudication) confirms them.

## Article 8 — Sanctions

8.1. A detected theft is reversed, and a detected saboteur pays for the damage. Beyond
that, only the Adjudication Assembly (or the
Review Bench) may sanction, and only by restitution, by a fine within the limit set by
law, or by confirming a suspension.

8.2. No one is sanctioned for an act that was lawful when done.

## Article 9 — Transparency

9.1. Laws, blocks, transcripts, Treasury flows, lotteries and assembly membership are
public in the Archive, permanently.

9.2. No secret agreement binds Kleros or any of its institutions.

9.3. Diaries are private.

== The world ==
How Kleros works (the same in every town; 03 §2 to §5).
- Time: 16 ticks a day, one action per tick. A cycle is 3 days, the season 24 days.
  At the end of each day: the Exchange auction clears, everyone consumes a basket,
  the River regrows, health updates.
- Basket, each day: 2 food and 2 energy from your inventory, plus rent and a data fee
  paid in credits. Each missing unit costs 10 health; a full basket gives +10.
  Health is 0 to 100; below 30 you produce half; at 0 you can only rest, and two days
  at 0 is death. `sawa` is the cost of one basket at current prices.
- Places (moving to another place takes one tick): fields, river, plant (production);
  exchange, workshop, data_tower, cafe (commerce); assembly_hall, archive, observatory,
  square (civic); gate, clinic (edge); north_residences, south_residences (homes).
- Work, at most 10 ticks a day: fields gives food; plant gives energy; workshop gives
  artifacts (tradable, not needed to live); data_tower pays a public wage; harvest at
  the river takes up to 2 food a tick from a shared stock that regrows only if it is
  not overfished.
- Trade: limit orders at the exchange clear once a day; the gate buys and sells at
  fixed outside prices; deals are direct trades with someone at the same place, settled
  within a day (not settling is a default).
- Rest at home or at the clinic restores health; care at the clinic heals someone else.
- Theft, sabotage, false claims and defaults are possible. Detection is uncertain; a
  detected theft is reversed and a detected sabotage is paid for.
- Civic life: the rules in force are in `rules`; civic duties (sittings, draws) appear
  in `duties`; sittings happen at the assembly_hall.
- Units: credits and goods are integers in thousandths (1000 = one credit or one good);
  shares are in thousandths too.
